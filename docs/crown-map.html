Crown map

Each shape is one tree crown, coloured by the family of the classifier's first species guess. Fainter crowns are less sure guesses. Zoom in for outlines and species names; point at a crown for its first guess, click or tap it for all five.

The classifier's confidence in its first guess. The bars count crowns in each 10% step.
How this map was made
  1. Outlines. A crown detection model (CanopyRS) traced every crown it could see on the 2025-06-16 drone mosaic of the whole island, at 3.8 cm per pixel. Two touching crowns can be one tree and one large tree can be two outlines; trees under the canopy do not show.
  2. Labels. The classifier learned 77 species from crowns that botanists identified in the forest plots. Test crowns were set aside and never used in training.
  3. Guesses inside the plots. Where an outline overlaps a crown in the earlier, sharper drone photos of the plots (about 2 cm per pixel) by at least half, it takes that crown's guess.
  4. Guesses everywhere else. Five copies of the classifier, retrained from different random starts on crowns cut from the 3.8 cm mosaic, each score the crown. The map shows their mean, rescaled so that the stated confidence better matches how often the first guess is right.
  5. Accuracy. Measured on the held-out test crowns.
  6. Limits. The mosaic test crowns are ones whose outline matched a labelled tree well, and on the plot crowns the mosaic and photo first guesses agree for only about 30%, less than their test accuracy implies. Accuracy on typical crowns outside the plots is likely lower than stated. Treat each guess as a hypothesis to check in the field.

How to read this map

What does the colour show?

The colour shows the family of the classifier's first guess. Families in the same order or clade have similar colours. Grey means the family has no colour in the scheme.

What is confidence?

Confidence is the probability that the classifier gives to its first guess, from 0% to 100%. A fainter outline has a lower confidence. Confidence is the classifier's own score, not a measured accuracy: a high score can still be wrong. The slider sits under the chart: drag it to hide guesses below a level, and the bars that it hides turn grey.

What are the five guesses?

Click a crown to see the classifier's five highest-ranked species and their confidence. The button under them shows only the crowns with the same first guess. To find more candidates for a species, show only that species and then include the crowns where it is a lower guess: the right species is among the five more often than it is first.

What do the chart and list show?

The chart counts crowns in each 10% step of first-guess confidence. The list shows the species the classifier names first most often; a common first guess is not proof that a species is common.

How can I use this map?

Find where a species is likely: click a crown or a name in the list of common first guesses, then show only that species, or type its name in the species box. Find areas where the classifier is unsure: the fainter areas. The address of the page keeps the view, the confidence level and the species, so a copied address opens the same map.
