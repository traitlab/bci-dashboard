What the columns mean

Top-1 accuracy on a species row is its recall: of the frames labelled that species, the share the first guess got right. Precision is the reverse: of the frames the model gave that name, the share that were it, counted only where a botanist labelled the frame. F1 is their harmonic mean, so a row scores well only when both do.

Model’s confidence is Pl@ntNet’s score for its first guess, averaged over the species’ frames. 0.86 means nearly all of it went on one name, and 0.32 means it was spread thin. Middle half is where the middle 50% of those frames fall, the 25th to the 75th percentile. A wide range is two behaviours averaged into one number, and the column sorts on the width.

What the statuses mean
  • Right name in the list, not first The right name was often among the 5 returned candidates but below the first guess. Check candidates against the photo.
  • Too few labels to judge Fewer than 10 labelled frames, so the score is too thin to trust yet.
  • Often wrong on labelled frames At least 10 labelled frames, but first guesses are right less than 70% of the time. Use manual identification or expert review.
  • Mixed Mixed results on labelled frames. Check each guess against the photo.
  • Usually right Usually right on labelled frames, so lower priority for checking model guesses. This does not measure the species' biodiversity importance.
  • Never returned on any BCI photo Pl@ntNet never returned this name on any BCI photo. We ask each photo for 5 names. A species it carries but never ranks that high looks the same to us as one it lacks. A 0.0% “Top-5 accuracy” under another status means the name did come back, just never on that species’ frames.
  • Not in the project's own species list Pl@ntNet's own list for this project does not carry it. Manual identification and biodiversity labels remain useful.

Each species gets the first status that fits. In order: “not in the project's own species list”, “usually right”, “right name in the list, not first”. Then “never returned on any BCI photo”, “too few labels to judge”. A few-frame species can still show as “right name in the list, not first”, since the candidate-list pattern can appear at any count.

A second score, on flights held back whole. The first guess is right on 86.7% of the 323 frames on the 9 held-back flights that carry an answer. These are not the test frames, so do not subtract one rate from the other.

Read on the same species, the two rates agree within their uncertainty. On the 30 species both sets hold, the first guess is right on 80.7% of 243 test frames and 87.9% of 313 held-back frames. Given the same mix of those species, it is 82.1% and 86.3%, held-back minus test +4.2 points. We are 95% sure the true gap is between −2.4 and +14.1 points.

On the test frames' own mix the gap is +3.7 points, 95% sure between −2.8 and +13.0 points. On the held-back flights' own mix the gap is +4.6 points, 95% sure between −3.6 and +15.3 points. All three mixes put the gap on the same side, but a range that includes zero leaves the direction of the gap unsettled.

How the same species mix is made

The headline mix counts each species by its frames in both sets added together, so neither set's mix is favoured. For each range we re-ran the count 10,000 times, each time drawing whole sites at random. Frames from one site are alike, and the two sets cover 11 sites. A drawn site brings its frames in both sets. In 9,474 of the 10,000 re-runs some species had no frame left in one set. That species was left out of the re-run, and the others' weights scaled up to fill its place. The seed is fixed so a rebuild prints the same range. Left out, because only one set holds them: 80 test frames on 35 species and 10 held-back frames on 7 species.

How the held-back flights were drawn

The v1 flight holdout holds 9 of the 43 flights back, whole. No train frame sits on any of them, so nothing counted there is a near-copy of a frame the labels already know. The two sets do not hold the same species in the same shares, so the two rates grade two different sets of frames.

92 species cannot be graded this way, because each one sits on a single flight. Holding that flight would leave the model nothing to learn the species from, so it stays in train.

How to read the numbers above

Read this when a number above surprises you.

What the centre crop costs, and how far that number reaches

Scoring the centre crop instead of a botanist’s outlines costs 6.3 points. Measured once, on 300 frames fixed in advance.

Which frames. 300 frames from 12 sites and 38 flight days, fixed before any of these numbers existed. Both ways of asking ran on every one.

What was compared. One Pl@ntNet call per outlined crown, pooled into one name for the frame by crown area. Against the fixed 1280×1280 square from the middle of a frame, 13.7% of the frame’s area, which every other rate on this page uses.

How sure. We are 95% sure the true gap is between +3.9 and +8.9 points. Frames from one site look alike, so the range comes from redrawing the 12 sites with replacement 10,000 times.

On 25 frames the outlines got the name right and the centre crop did not. On 6 it went the other way.

How far the gap reaches.

  • One export batch. Every frame here carries the earlier file naming, zoom. Later flights were exported under the other naming.
  • 12 of the 17 field sites. The other five are unknowns.
  • Per frame, not per species. The two commonest of its 72 species are 26.3% of the 300 frames, so the gap leans towards what the model knows best.

Which frames, what counts as right and when we could look were all fixed before the data. The full read and the warnings the design requires are in its writeup, sent on request.

Why the two headline scores differ: the same frames, four rates

56.0% per species and 78.5% per frame, on the same frames.

Every rate here scores a centre crop against a label for the whole frame. The two are not always looking at the same tree. On 913 of 2,554 scored frames the labelled species covers less than half the crop, and on 105 it covers none of it. So a wrong answer is not always a wrong identification.

Per species asks how many kinds of tree the model can name, which is what a labelling programme moves. Per frame asks how often it is right on a photo picked at random, which the commonest species decide.

What these four are measured on

Why the per-frame figure is one number. Each frame has one botanist label and one first guess. So a wrong guess is one miss for one species and one false alarm for another. Both denominators are the frame count, so precision, recall, F1 and top-1 accuracy come out identical.

Precision is over the frames we scored. A false alarm only shows where a botanist labelled the frame, so these are rates over the frames we scored, not over the survey. The averages run over the 93 labelled species only.

A species the model never guesses scores 0% precision, not a blank, so an empty list does not flatter the average. Rows on few frames are noisy too, which is why a row under 10 labelled frames starts hidden in the species table.

Read the two share columns against each other. The 13 single-frame species carry 14% of the per-species vote and 1% of the per-frame one. They are also the row Pl@ntNet gets right least often, 7.7% against 81.7% at 25 or more frames. That is the gap between the two headline rates.

Misses differ at each end. On species with 4 frames or fewer, the right name is still in the 5 for 29.7% of 37 misses. At 25+ it is 42.2% of 389. Misses on common species are near misses; on rare ones the model does not know the plant.

Set aside species under 10 frames and the scores become 80.6% per frame and 74.7% per species. That is 6 points apart, not the 23 between 78.5% and 56.0%. A one-frame species scores only 0% or 100%, so those 13 votes are coin flips.

Read those rows as how common a species is, not as something labelling changed. The model dates from 2026-03-20 (7.5) and our labels did not move it. Common species have more reference photos inside Pl@ntNet. What extra labels buy is a rate steady enough to act on, which takes about 10 frames.

Is the confidence score worth anything: in bulk yes, on rare species no

How often the first guess is right at each confidence score.

The counts behind every band, over all 2,554 labelled frames, are in confidence_calibration.csv. Higher bands are right more often, which is why ordering the label queue by confidence works.

It is not a probability. A band right 95.2% of the time is not a score of 0.9. And it holds in bulk only: on species with few labelled frames a high score means much less, as the Labelling queue shows.

Trusting a frame only when few names are plausible. A separate check over the 1,055 labelled frames the model has a view of, 71 species.

What the words mean: frame, label, crown, centre crop

Four words do all the work on this page.

  • A frame is one 4000×3000 drone photo.
  • A crown is one tree canopy a botanist outlined inside a frame.
  • A frame's label is the species whose outlined crowns cover the most area in the whole frame.
  • The centre crop is the fixed 1280×1280 square from the middle of a frame, 13.7% of the frame’s area. Every rate over all the labelled frames is scored on it. We ask Pl@ntNet for 5 names per photo, a setting of ours and not a limit of the model.
  • The first guess is the top-ranked of those names, and right means it matches the frame's label.
  • Outlining the trees first means asking Pl@ntNet about each crown on its own. The answers are then weighted by how much of the frame each crown covers. The label is built the same way, so this is the fairer comparison.
Why three different frame counts: 2,554, 2,554 and 2,584

They do not contradict each other. Each counts a different thing.

2,554 frames carry a label naming a species. Every accuracy rate on this page is measured on those.

2,554 frames have a cached Pl@ntNet answer: the 2,554 above, plus 0 labelled only to a genus and 0 labelled only to a family.

2,584 frames a botanist has labelled at all, the 30 with no cached answer included.

Species the labels have reached: 93, over the 2,584 of 2,584 labelled frames that name a species. 12 were seen once and 10 twice. By the Chao1 rule that suggests about 7 more not yet reached, so the list is about 93% complete. Every labelled frame counts here, cached answer or not.

Since the last snapshot: no earlier snapshot is available for this build. Today 46 species carry at least 10 labelled frames, and the first guess is right on 75.9% of the 323 test frames.

How this was measured: the model, the frames, the files

Read this before quoting any number outside the team.

  • Predictions: model run k-central-america@2026-03-20 (7.5), the Central America regional model, so a regional restriction is already in place. That restriction excludes 3 species BCI has recorded, checked against the region’s own species list.
  • Model version: Pl@ntNet reports the model of 20 March 2026 (release 7.5), unchanged on 7 September 2026. BCI labels were first sent to Pl@ntNet in July 2026, after that date, so none is in it. Whether BCI photos reached it some other way, we cannot say. The 2,554 cached answers predate the version field. Every dated answer we hold names this same model.
  • Request: the settings at the foot of this page, on a 1280 px centre crop, organs detected automatically. A correct answer at position 6 or beyond was never returned and cannot be seen here.
  • Evaluated set: 2,554 frames across 93 species whose botanist label names a species rather than only a genus. They are what was labelled, not a random draw. The rates carry over to unlabelled frames only if those look like the labelled ones, which cannot be checked offline.
  • Labels: merged from the Labelbox export of 6 August 2026. The merge keeps the newer label, and that batch has had no Labelbox review step yet.
  • Snapshot: one dated folder, the latest state, with no trend over earlier folders. Every number is recomputed at build time and checked against the 9 tables the measurement pass wrote. A mismatch aborts the build.
Pl@ntNet was asked the same way every time: five answers per photo, rejection off, related images off. Run k-central-america@2026-03-20 (7.5). The rare-species ordering was measured with labelfirst 0.9.0, against the file of photo vectors f64c62db927e.
